JobFairX — Job Seeker Experience

Interview types audit

What must change on the Job Seeker side now that interviews can be JobFairX video, phone, in person, or on the employer's own meeting link — audited against the shipped Employer implementation.

August 16, 2026 · Audit only, nothing implemented · Evidence gathered from the live files, not from documentation

1 Executive summary

The dashboard is already done. It shipped on August 15 in two commits and supports all four types, including the employer's external link. The current handoff document says this work is unpushed and blocked; that is stale. I ran the visual QA it says was never done — four methods across three phases, twelve combinations, zero defects.

Everything downstream of the dashboard is not done. Three surface groups still assume every interview is JobFairX video:

  • The interview experience — the largest gap by far. 65 camera/microphone references in interview-flow.html, 19 more in screen-preview.html, and zero type awareness in either. For phone and in person there is no seeker-side day-of experience at all.
  • The six seeker emails — zero type awareness. Two of them are actively wrong for three of the four types.
  • Marketing copy — the "How it works" panel promises "No Zoom, no downloads," which the external-link type now contradicts outright.
The single most important finding

The seeker side has no answer to a simple question: it is 11:00 AM and my interview is a phone interview — what do I look at? Today the answer is a dashboard row. There is no day-of experience, no arrival screen, no call screen. Video gets a green room, a camera test, a background picker, a waiting room and a feedback flow. The other three types get a table cell.

Two structural recommendations sit alongside the type work: a redesign of the Actions area (§7), and a correction to the interview-type control (§8) — where I am recommending something different from what was asked, for a reason I want to put in front of you before anything is built.

Constraint that shapes the plan

Decision D-7 froze the desktop interview flow. Any non-video day-of experience is new screens, not modifications of the frozen ones — which is convenient, because a phone interview should never render a video UI anyway. I am treating the freeze as intact and additive-only. Confirm if that has changed.

The concept that should govern all of it

The employer side is organised around one idea, and it transfers to the seeker side exactly. What the platform can observe determines what it should display.

TypePlatform can observeConsequence for the seeker UI
JobFairX videoJoined, started, endedReal-time state. "Join" is meaningful. Waiting room works.
Employer's linkNothing after handoff"Join" opens someone else's product. JobFairX cannot know it started or ended.
PhoneNothingNo join. The seeker waits for a ring. Everything is declared, not detected.
In personNothingNo join. The seeker travels. Address and timing are the whole product.

This is why "add a type field to the existing screens" is the wrong shape of fix for the interview experience. Three of the four types don't need a modified video screen — they need a different screen, or no screen.

3 Screen-by-screen recommendations

Dashboard — upcoming events

Type-aware already. Remaining work is the Actions area (§7) and the type control (§8). No changes needed to the method line itself; the labels read well and match the employer vocabulary.

Day-of experience — the new work

Four distinct destinations, not one screen with a type flag.

TypeDestinationWhat it contains
JobFairX videoExisting flow, unchangedGreen room, camera test, backgrounds, waiting room, in-call, feedback. Do not touch.
Employer's linkHandoff screen (new)Platform name, the meeting link as a large primary action, "this opens Microsoft Teams," a short "what to expect," and a JobFairX-side Mark as finished — because we cannot observe the end. No camera test: we cannot test someone else's room.
PhoneCall screen (new)The number the employer will call, the time, "keep your ringer on," and an explicit wrong number? route to profile. No join. Should be reachable but is mostly a reassurance surface.
In personArrival screen (new)Map, address, directions as primary, parking, arrival instructions, what to bring, and the time. Must be mobile-first — this is read in a car or on a sidewalk.
A seeker-side gap worth solving properly

For the three unobservable types, JobFairX never learns the interview happened. The employer declares it on their side. The seeker's dashboard will sit on "Interview confirmed" forever unless something moves it. Recommend a lightweight seeker-side confirmation — one tap, "Did your interview happen?" — which also gives you the feedback hook you already have for video. Without it, Interviewed and Missed become employer-only truth and the seeker's history is wrong.

Green room / get-ready

Scope it explicitly to JobFairX video. For the employer's link it is misleading (we would be testing the wrong camera); for phone and in person it is meaningless.

4 Workflow changes

What does not change

Register → matched → request → employer accepts → interview. The type never alters the funnel. Request, accept, decline, propose-new-times and reschedule are all type-neutral and should stay that way. This mirrors the employer side, where Accept / Decline / Reschedule was the one workflow that needed nothing.

What does change

Point in flowChange
MatchThe type is already known — it is an event-level setting the employer chooses at setup. Show it from the match email onward, not from confirmation.
Request submittedState the type. In person shows city and state only; the full address is withheld until confirmed.
ConfirmedFull details unlock: address plus parking and arrival, or the meeting link, or the callback number.
Day-ofRoutes to one of four destinations instead of one. Phone and in person have no "join" event at all.
CompletionVideo self-completes. The other three need a declared end — see the box in §3.
Already resolved, carry it forward

Interview type is set per event by the employer during setup and is overridable per interview. That answers the open question in the current handoff about whether the type is known at match time. It is. Matched and Requested rows should show a real type, never a dash.

5 UI improvements beyond type support

Destructive actions are undifferentiated — fix this first

In the confirmed state, Cancel interview and Unregister render with identical styling, side by side. One cancels a single interview. The other removes the seeker from the entire hiring event, including every other match at it. A seeker trying to drop one interview can plausibly leave the whole fair. This is a real defect, independent of interview types.

Action density swings 1 → 7

Measured across states: Waiting to be matched and Request declined carry one action; Interview confirmed carries seven. The column is sized for the worst case and looks broken at the best case. §7 addresses this.

Other findings

  • Status column is doing two jobs. It carries the time, the status pill, and now the method line. It reads well today but will not absorb a fourth thing. Worth watching, not fixing yet.
  • Mobile. The table collapses to a card layout and holds up — I verified it. The in-person day-of case is the one to design for explicitly: it is the only screen read while physically moving.
  • Accessibility. Actions are a mix of <a> and <button> with no consistent semantics, and the overflow uses a text link with a glyph rather than a real disclosure control. Fix as part of §7 rather than separately.
  • Em dashes. The no-dash rule is unenforceable as stated — this page has 12 em dashes in shipped copy, and Search Jobs and the employer emails have more. Recommend scoping the rule to the interview flow, where it originated.

Email 5 — verbatim, and why it fails

INTERVIEW LOCATION on JobFairX Interview opens in your browser. No download needed.

The label is right — it already says "Interview location," matching the employer app. The value is hardcoded, and the browser promise is false for phone, in person, and the employer's link. Each type needs its own confirmation payload: address plus parking and arrival, or the meeting link and platform, or the callback number.

Email 6 — verbatim, and why it fails

Subject: Your Nike interview is starting. Join now CTA: Join interview → Footnote: Opens in your browser. No download needed.

For phone there is nothing to join — the seeker's phone rings, and an email telling them to click is actively confusing. For in person they are already at the venue and this email arrives too late to be useful. For the employer's link, "opens in your browser" is technically true but points at Teams, not JobFairX.

Recommended per type: video keeps it as-is; employer's link becomes "Your interview is starting — open Microsoft Teams" with the link as the CTA; phone becomes "Nike is calling you now — keep your phone nearby," no CTA; in person should send earlier as a leave-now nudge rather than a start-now alert, or not send at all.

Sequencing note

The seeker emails are the phase after the seeker app in your stated order. The findings are here so the audit is complete, but I would not build them until the day-of experience exists — email 6 has to point somewhere, and for three of the four types that destination does not exist yet.

Marketing

"When the employer accepts, you get a confirmed time and interview by video right on JobFairX. No Zoom, no downloads."

Now false. External-link events run on Zoom, Teams, Meet or Webex by design. Recommended: "When the employer accepts, you get a confirmed time and everything you need — whether that's a video call on JobFairX, a phone call, or directions to the door." Keeps the reassurance, drops the promise we break.

Six of the seven confirmed-state actions are undifferentiated text links. One button, then a flat list in which "Message employer" and "Unregister from the event" carry identical visual weight.

Recommendation — three tiers, and reuse the employer pattern

TierTreatmentContents
PrimaryOne filled button, type-awareJoin interview (video) · Open Microsoft Teams (link) · Get directions (in person) · none (phone — a reassurance card instead, which is already how it works)
SupportingAt most two inline linksType-dependent: Test camera & mic for JobFairX video only; Message employer once confirmed
OverflowIcon button opening a menuReschedule · Cancel interview · Unregister — with the destructive pair visually separated and scope-labelled
Why an overflow menu rather than cards or split buttons

Reuse, not invention. The employer lobby solved this exact problem: a primary action plus a kebab menu sitting side by side in one row. Adopting the same shape is the single biggest consistency win available between the two products, and it comes with a tested interaction rather than a new one.

Split buttons are wrong here — the primary action differs per type and for phone there is no primary action at all. A split button whose main half is empty is broken by construction.

Action cards are too heavy for a table row that already carries an event, a job, a status, a time and a method line. They would win on a detail page, not here.

The destructive-scope fix belongs in this redesign. Inside the menu, Cancel interview and Unregister get separated by a divider, and Unregister reads with its scope attached — it removes you from the event, not the interview. That is a one-line copy change that closes the defect in §5.

Net effect on the confirmed state: seven flat items become one button, up to two links, and one menu. The 1-action states stay clean because the menu only appears when there is something in it.

8 Dashboard and the interview-type control

I am recommending against what was asked — here is why

The brief asks to move the interview-type control out of Demo States and onto the dashboard "so it feels like part of the product rather than a development/testing feature."

It cannot become part of the product. The job seeker has no authority over their interview type — the employer sets it per event during setup. A control that looks like product UI tells the seeker they can switch a video interview to a phone interview. They cannot. The best case is that they try and nothing happens; the worst case is that they believe they changed it and turn up to the wrong thing.

What I recommend instead

Keep it a preview control, but fix the two real problems with it — it is buried, and it is in the wrong place conceptually.

  • Move it to the top of the dashboard, above the table, where you will actually find it. This satisfies the discoverability half of the ask.
  • Label it as a preview, not a setting. "Preview as: JobFairX video / Employer's link / Phone / In person." The word preview does the work.
  • Style it as scaffolding — the muted, obviously-not-product treatment the employer prototype already uses for its prototype-only strip. Same convention across both products.
  • Remove it from Demo States so there is exactly one place it lives.

This gets you everything the brief actually wants — easy to find, sensibly placed, no longer buried in a dev panel — without shipping a control that lies about who is in charge.

If you want it to feel like product anyway

There is an honest version: a read-only type filter — "show me only my in-person interviews" — which is a real seeker need once someone has several interviews across several events. That is a genuine product feature and it is discoverable in the same position. It just does not do what the demo toggle does. Say the word and I will spec it separately.

Other dashboard recommendations

  • Leave the method line in the Status column. It works, it is verified, and moving it to its own column now would break a shipped design for no user benefit.
  • Past events should keep the type. "How did I interview for that one?" is a real question when a seeker is comparing three employers.
  • The multi-interview scenario is where type visibility pays off most — one event, several interviews, potentially different types. Worth QA-ing explicitly once the action redesign lands.
