JobFairX — Job Seeker Experience

Navigation and information architecture review

Whether Events and Interviews should become separate workspaces — reviewed against the Employer application's own IA and the patterns used by Indeed, LinkedIn, Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby.

August 16, 2026 · Review only, nothing implemented · Measured from both running applications

1 Executive summary

Your instinct is right, and the Employer application already proves it. I read its navigation directly rather than assuming — the employer app runs on Events · Candidates · Interviews · Messages · Automations · Analytics, with no Dashboard at all, and its Interviews section already uses Upcoming / Pending / Past — the exact three categories you proposed. The seeker app is the outlier, not the thing that needs inventing.

The specific defect underneath the confusion

The seeker dashboard's tabs read "Upcoming events" and "Past events" — but the content is interviews. The labels say one object and the rows show another. That single mislabel is most of the cognitive load you are feeling, and no amount of visual polish fixes it.

There is also a half-built Events concept already: the sidebar's Job fairs item calls openEvents(), which opens a modal, while a view-events section exists in the markup. Events are simultaneously a nav item, a modal, and a column inside the interviews table.

The insight that reorganises everything

"Waiting to be matched" and "Matched" are not interview states. They are event states. No interview exists yet — that is precisely why those rows have empty Job details and, until recently, offered nothing but Unregister. They were being forced into an interviews table because there was nowhere else to put them.

Split Events from Interviews and they land naturally: an Interview object only exists once the candidate has requested one. Everything before that belongs to the event registration.

Recommended

Drop Dashboard. You asked whether it still adds value — it does not, for three reasons:

  • The employer app already dropped it and is the more complex product. If an employer managing five events and eighty candidates does not need a summary screen, a candidate with one to three interviews certainly does not.
  • It would only ever restate Events and Interviews. A dashboard earns its place when it aggregates across many objects. Here it would show the same two cards you are one click from anyway.
  • It is the thing causing the conflation. "Dashboard" is what allowed events, interviews, matching states and invitations to pile into one table.
Where the candidate lands on sign-in

Not a fixed page — route on state. Any interviews at all → Interviews, because that is what they logged in for. No interviews → Events, which is where the next useful action is. This is what LinkedIn does with My Jobs and what Ashby does with a candidate's interview list.

Keep Messages and Profile as they are — both match the employer app. JobAgentX stays as an external link, visually separated.

The consequence to be deliberate about

Under this model, "Matched" produces no interview row. It is an invitation sitting on the event. Only once the candidate requests does an interview appear, in Pending. This is cleaner, but it means the Events page must make invitations impossible to miss — otherwise a match could sit unnoticed. My recommendation is a count badge on the Events nav item and an accent-bordered invitation block on the event card.

The one action, and where it goes

Unregister from this event belongs here, in the event card's overflow menu — not on an interview, which is what we corrected earlier. This page is where it finally has a natural home.

5 Interviews workspace

Three tabs — Upcoming · Pending · Past — with counts, matching the employer app exactly.

Empty state

Headline "You do not have any interviews yet", then the causal explanation: interviews become available only after an employer matches you and invites you to request one. Primary CTA Browse events, plus a quiet secondary link to your registered events if any exist — because if they are registered and waiting, "browse more events" is not actually their next step.

Interview card

The card built in the current redesign already carries employer, job title, date, time, interview type, status and contextual next steps across all four interview types. It ports over unchanged. Two additions for this context:

  • Event name on the card — necessary once interviews are no longer grouped under events.
  • Group by day inside Upcoming once there are several, the way Ashby and Greenhouse do. Below three, a flat list is fine.
A defect in what I shipped this session

You have specified that candidates cannot reschedule — they only accept one of the employer's proposed times or decline. The interview card I built includes "Reschedule interview" in its overflow menu. That is wrong and needs removing. Cancel stays; reschedule goes.

6 Job details — keep the modal, add a second door

The overflow menu should stay, but it should not be the only way in. A menu is where you look for actions, not for information — which is why "View job details" hiding behind ⋯ tests poorly.

Recommendation: make the job title itself the primary affordance, and keep the menu item as a secondary path. Clicking a job title to read the job is the pattern in every product in the benchmark set. The modal built this session already carries the description, employer information, event, interview type, interview details and the requested or scheduled time, and it is state-aware and type-aware. It needs no redesign — only a second entry point, which is already wired on the job title in the current build.

Your six steps are right. The failure today is not the wording, it is that the explanation appears after registration, when the expectation has already been set wrongly.

Wording worth being careful with

Step 3 of your list says "JobFairX matches candidates to employer job openings" and step 4 says "Employers review matched candidates." Those describe two different actors deciding. Candidates will read "JobFairX matched me" as a guarantee and then feel misled when nothing follows. I would phrase it as: JobFairX suggests candidates who fit each role, and the employer decides who they want to interview. Same process, no implied promise.

8 UX concerns with the current design

  • Tabs mislabelled. "Upcoming events" lists interviews. Resolved by the split.
  • Events exist in three forms — a nav item, a modal (openEvents()), and a column. Resolved by making Events a real page.
  • Reschedule is offered to candidates and should not be. Live defect.
  • Non-interview states rendered as interviews, which is why they look broken.
  • No way to see who is attending an event. The main thing a candidate wants before registering is absent.
  • Resume completeness is invisible — the only lever the candidate controls.
  • Design system debt persists — 18 font sizes, 66 colours, 13 radii. Worth folding into this rebuild rather than doing separately.
