The event-day queue — how candidates move

How a candidate travels from scheduled to interviewed for the three interview locations JobFairX cannot observe: in person, phone, and video on the employer’s own meeting link. What each state means, what moves a candidate out of it, and who does the moving. JobFairX video works differently and is contrasted throughout.

One list, not two rooms

There is no waiting room and no interview room here, because there is no room. Those are literal on JobFairX video — a room the candidate enters and the employer joins. On a phone call, in a physical lobby, or in a Teams meeting, neither word points at anything.

Every row in the list carries both the primary action and the row menu. Splitting them across two sections put the button in one place and the only escape hatch in the other, and buried whoever had waited longest underneath people who had just arrived.

No-show is the fifth destination and it is terminal. A candidate reaches it from the waiting list, never from Interviewing.

JobFairX video is the exception. There the Waiting-to-interview tab still holds two sections — Interview rooms for candidates who are in a room with an interviewer free, status Ready, and Waiting rooms for those in a room with nobody free yet, status Awaiting available interviewer. Both are computed from things the platform can see. None of that is available anywhere else.

2What the platform can actually see

This is the single fact that explains every difference below.

PhoneTold — candidate checks inTold — employer says soTold

Video, external linkTold — candidate checks inToldTold
External-link video belongs with phone and in person, not with JobFairX video. The interview happens in Teams or Zoom. JobFairX cannot see who joined, when it started, or when it finished — exactly as with a phone call or a handshake. It is a video interview by method and an unobservable one by mechanism, so its queue is driven by people telling it things.

Where the platform observes nothing, every transition needs a person to declare it, and every state needs a manual way out.

3Check-in is the only signal

One fact moves a candidate into the queue, and it is known by exactly one party.

SignalWho knows itWhat it does
The candidate is hereCandidateMoves them out of Not yet interviewed and into Waiting to interview. In person this means “I’ve arrived”; on phone and external link it means “I’m ready.” Same signal, same destination.
The interview is startingEmployerMoves the row to Interviewing. Only ever the employer, only ever explicit.
Arrival is the candidate’s to declare; starting is the employer’s. The employer cannot know whether someone has walked through the door or is sitting by their phone. The candidate cannot know when a recruiter is free. Each party reports only what they can see, and the candidate’s only action in the whole flow is check-in — after that they can put their phone away.
There is no promotion step, and no availability control. On JobFairX video the platform promotes a candidate from Waiting rooms to Interview rooms when it can see both that they are present and that an interviewer is free, and the employer sets Your status to Available or Away to feed it. Here neither half exists: the recruiter knows they are free because they are the one standing up, and the candidate is told by a name being called or a phone ringing. A self-reported availability flag would be reporting to a queue nobody is reading, so Your status does not appear on these three.

4In person

  1. The candidate arrives at the venue and checks in Candidate → Waiting to interview, status Checked in.
  2. The recruiter looks at the list, walks out and collects whoever they want next.
  3. When that person sits down, the recruiter presses Start interview Employer. The row moves to Interviewing in place — no new window, no modal. The interview is happening in the room, not on the screen.
  4. End interview → confirm → rate → Interviewed.
The list is ordered by how long someone has been waiting, longest first. At a venue that is the number that matters — a candidate sitting in your lobby for forty minutes is a problem you want at the top of the screen, not buried under people who arrived moments ago. The column is headed Waiting.

There is no out-of-order override, because there is no order to override. The recruiter starts whoever is in front of them.

5Phone

  1. The candidate signals they are ready for the call by checking in Candidate → Waiting to interview.
  2. The recruiter presses Call candidate. A panel opens beside the row with the number, a copy button and a tap-to-dial link.
  3. The recruiter dials from their own phone, then presses I’ve started the interview Employer → Interviewing.
  4. End interview → rate → Interviewed.
Opening the call panel changes nothing. It is not the start of the interview. Employers open it to copy a number, to check they have the right one, or to look again after leaving a voicemail. Only the explicit I’ve started the interview moves the candidate. Treating the panel as the trigger would report interviews that never happened.

Nobody answers

The most common phone failure, and it is deliberately two-strike:

  1. First No answer — the candidate stays in the list carrying a “No answer · N attempts” line, and the button on their row becomes Call again. They are still a live candidate.
  2. Second No answer — the employer is now offered Mark as no-show.

Employers routinely call twice. Ending someone on the first miss would record reachable candidates as unreachable, which is a worse error than an extra click.

6Video on an external link

  1. The meeting link is set when the event is created. It appears in the lobby header with a Copy link control — that is where the employer gets it on the day.
  2. The candidate checks in Candidate → Waiting to interview. They received the same link with their confirmation.
  3. The recruiter presses Start interview Employer. The row moves to Interviewing in place. No JobFairX room opens — the conversation is in Teams or Zoom, outside the product.
  4. End interview → rate → Interviewed.

What the lobby drops

Everything that only means something inside a JobFairX room:

  • No Your status control. Nothing on this screen decides when a candidate is admitted to a Teams meeting.
  • No Update display name. The name labels you inside the JobFairX room; the candidate sees whatever Teams shows.
  • No Test video and audio. That test exercises the JobFairX video stack, which this interview never touches.
  • No Rejoin. There is no JobFairX room to rejoin. The link stays in the header with Copy link if the employer drops out.
The chip still reads Video, and the button still says Start interview with the camcorder icon. An external-link event is a video interview — the method has not changed, only the mechanism. What changed is what the button does.

7Every way out of the queue

Where the platform observes nothing, a candidate can get stuck. Nothing tells JobFairX that a phone candidate hung up, that someone in the waiting area gave up and went home, or that an interview happened entirely off-screen. So every row carries a menu alongside its button.

ExitAvailable onWhat it is for
Mark as interviewedEvery row, all threeAn interview that happened while nobody touched the app — routine when the employer is talking rather than typing. Asks who interviewed them and the actual time, and will not confirm until both are given.
Didn’t showEvery row, all threeThe manual no-show, for a candidate who never turned up or stopped responding.
No answer ×2Phone onlyReached from the call panel rather than the row menu. The second one offers no-show.
This menu is not a convenience. On JobFairX video the platform can watch someone leave the room and clean up after them. On these three it cannot. Remove the menu and the only way a candidate ever leaves the list is by being interviewed — so the queue never empties and the event never reconciles.

Ending an interview, and the second option

End interview asks whether the employer has finished with that candidate:

  • Yes → the rating modal opens, pre-filled with any note already taken during the interview, and the candidate moves to Interviewed.
  • No → the candidate goes back to Waiting to interview. On phone the option reads “…so you can call again”; on in person and external link, “…Start again when you’re ready.”

A returning candidate joins the list with their wait reset, so they sit at the bottom of an order that runs longest-wait-first.
