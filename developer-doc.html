1What changed, and the language for it

JobFairX runs virtual hiring events. Employers register for an event, post jobs, and interview candidates on event day. The app supported video interviews only. It now supports three interview locations: Video, Phone and In person.

The picker for choosing one already existed in Edit Post and Setup Flow. Nothing downstream read it — not the dashboard, not the lobby, not the interview room, not a single email. That is what this work fixed.

Language rules

  • Candidates request interviews. They never “apply.” An employer accepting a request schedules the interview.
  • Say Video when you mean the method. “Virtual” is no longer a synonym for JobFairX — it is one of three options.
  • “Join” is video-only. Never use it for phone or in person, and never in a string shared across all three.
  • The concept is Interview location. A physical address is an Interview address. “Desired location” means where the candidate wants to work and is a different thing.

Design rules this was built under

  1. Reuse existing components. Don't invent new patterns. The lobby and Interviews pages are the source of truth.
  2. Don't change video. Everything is gated so a video-only employer sees no difference — with two deliberate exceptions, noted in §12.
  3. The verb says what the employer does; the icon and chip say how. Two verbs across three methods: Start interview and Call candidate.
  4. One honest string beats three conditional ones. Only branch copy where a single wording would be false for some method.
  5. Don't show a promise the product doesn't keep.
The test to design against. An employer who only ever runs phone interviews should use JobFairX for a month and never meet a word, icon, button or status that assumes a camera — and an employer who only ever runs video should not notice anything changed.

2Interview location — where it's set

LevelSet inBehaviour
Event defaultInterview settings, in Edit Post and Setup FlowApplies to every interview for that event unless the interview overrides it.

An interview's location can differ from its event's. Any list that spans more than one event shows the location on every row, not once at the top.

Changing an event's location after publication

One decision for the whole confirmed batch, not per candidate:

  • Keep confirmed candidates on their original location — this legitimately produces a mixed event.
  • Move everyone and notify them.

Mixed events are real and supported.

The A/V test prompt is conditional

“Test your audio and video before your event” shows only when an upcoming event uses JobFairX video. It used to be permanent and account-level, so a phone-only employer was nagged forever to test hardware they never use.

Interviews tab

Columns: Name | Desired job | Desired location | Interview time | Interview location | Action.

This is the one view that genuinely mixes methods — interviews across all events, on Upcoming / Pending / Past. The Interview location column is always present, even on a single-method programme. A column that appears and disappears depending on whether an event happens to be mixed is more confusing than one that is simply always there.

Candidates tab

Candidate-centric, filtered by stage. The location chip appends to the existing stage · time line beneath each candidate's name in the All view, reusing that slot rather than adding a column.

In the candidate drawer, the no-show reason is worded per location (§11), and the primary action for a candidate scheduled today uses that location's icon rather than always the camcorder.

4The lobby

Event header

A line under the date range: the chip plus a description, shown for every location. In person adds a Parking & arrival link opening a modal with the address, parking and arrival instructions — the first place the employer can read back what they entered in Edit Post.

An external-link event shows the platform name, the meeting URL and a Copy link control here. That is where the employer gets the link on the day.

On the single list, every row carries both the primary action and the row menu. On JobFairX video the primary action is on the Interview-rooms rows and there is no row menu at all.

The row menu isn't a convenience

Nothing tells the app that a phone, in-person or external-link candidate has left. This menu is the only way they ever leave the list without being interviewed.

  • Mark as interviewed — records an interview the app never saw. Asks for the interviewer and the actual time; the confirm button stays disabled until both are filled.
  • Didn't show — the manual no-show.

There is no out-of-order override, because with one list there is no order to override — the recruiter starts whoever they want.

The Notes column appears on phone, in person and external link, between Interviewer and Action. JobFairX video takes notes inside its own room, so it has no Notes column.

Your status — JobFairX video only

The Your status: Available / Away control in the live header, and the “Ready to start interviewing?” card it drives, appear on JobFairX video events only.

The reason, so it doesn't look like an oversight: the other three have no promotion step for an availability flag to gate. The candidate checks in, lands in the list, and the next thing that happens is physical — the recruiter walks out and collects them, or dials them, or opens Teams. Nothing on this screen decides when a candidate is admitted, and nothing here reaches them.

5Phone

The Call panel

An anchored popover, deliberately not a modal, so the queue stays visible while dialling. It contains:

  • Candidate name and role.
  • The phone number, large and selectable, with a Copy button and a tap-to-dial link.
  • The line: “Call {first name} from your phone. JobFairX doesn't place the call — candidates see the number you call from.”
  • The scheduled time.
  • I've started the interview (primary), No answer, Close, and Number looks wrong? which opens the message composer.
Opening the panel changes nothing. Only I've started the interview moves the candidate to Interviewing. Employers open this to copy a number, to check it, or after leaving a voicemail — opening a panel is not evidence an interview began.

No answer is two-strike

The first press returns the candidate to the queue with a “No answer · N attempts” sub-line and flips the button to Call again. The second press offers Mark as no-show. Employers routinely call twice; one strike would report reachable candidates as unreachable.

6In person

  • Start interview moves the row in place. No new window, no modal.
  • Mark as interviewed records an interview the app never saw.
  • The Parking & arrival modal in the lobby header shows the address, parking instructions and arrival instructions entered in Edit Post.
  • The schedule modal blocks Send until street, city, state and ZIP are complete.

7Notes

JobFairX video takes notes inside its interview room. Phone, in person and external-link events never open that room, so before this work their only surface was the Interviewed tab — after the interview had ended. An employer interviewing in person with a laptop had nowhere to write.

  • A Notes column on the Interviewing tab opens the resume with a notes panel beside it.
  • Side by side is the primary layout, because being able to read and write at once is the entire point. Tabs alone would cut clicks without letting you see both.
  • Below 1100px the panel collapses into Resume / Notes tabs as the responsive fallback.
  • JobFairX video is untouched — no panel, no tabs. It keeps its in-room notes.
  • The small notes modal is kept for the Interviewed tab. A quick after-the-fact edit doesn't need the resume.
  • The resume modal header was reduced from 178px to 79px, taking resume visibility from 60% to 82% of the modal.
A note belongs to the candidate, not to the row. A note taken mid-interview must follow that person — into the Interviewed tab, into the candidate drawer, and into any later session.

8Ending an interview

End interview asks “Have you finished interviewing {name}?”

AnswerResult
YesThe row moves to Interviewed carrying the rating, the interviewer, the actual time, and the note. Tab counts follow.
NoThe candidate goes back to Waiting to interview, rejoining the list with their wait reset. On JobFairX video the original wording and destination are unchanged.

The rating modal pre-fills whatever note already exists for that candidate, relabelled Interview notes, with a line saying where it came from. It must never open with the notes field blank — that destroys notes taken during the interview.

Ratings are Yes / Maybe / No. JobFairX doesn't track hires, so the recruiter's rating is the outcome measure.

9Schedule & reschedule

Two columns. Left: a Format segmented control — Video / Phone / In-Person — with that format's fields underneath, then Message. Right: Interview Date, Start Time, Suggest multiple times.

FormatFields shown
VideoJobFairX video call (Recommended), or Third-party video conferencing which reveals Meeting URL.
PhoneA note only: “When {name} confirms, we send you their phone number. Call them at the scheduled time.”
In personThe full address set — street, suite, city, state, ZIP — plus Parking instructions and Arrival instructions. Same labels and example text as Edit Post, so there is one address format across the app.
  • The picker opens on the event's location.
  • One format applies to the whole proposal, not per suggested time slot.
  • Send is blocked until the in-person address is complete.
  • The modal holds one fixed size across all three formats — top and height stay put, and the left column scrolls when in person overflows. It must not resize or jump when the format changes.
  • Deliberately excluded: Duration and Time zone. The event already fixes both, and asking again per interview would let one interview contradict its own event.

10Edit Post & Setup Flow

  • Both carry the interview location picker — a segmented Video / Phone / In-Person control with fields per option.
  • Video reveals JobFairX video call or Third-party video conferencing with a required Meeting URL.
  • In person reveals the full address set, validated for completeness.
  • Edit Post carries the change-location modal — keep confirmed candidates, or move everyone.
  • Setup Flow's review page shows an Interview location row. It previously omitted it entirely, so the last screen before publishing hid the setting with the highest cost of being wrong.

Writing conventions

Sound human. Casual-confident. No AI phrases. Buttons name the action, not the channel. Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it. Empty states say what to do next, not what to wait for.

12Look and feel

The location chip

One treatment for all three: #8a5a20 on #f6efe6. Sized to match the status badge exactly. Three fixed icons — camcorder, handset, map-pin. A larger variant is used in the lobby header.

Why one colour. Colour in this app means state — success, warning, danger, ready. Three per-location hues had borrowed those palettes for something that carries no state at all: phone read as “good”, in person as “caution”, video as “ready”, and each one collided exactly with an existing status colour. The icon carries the location now; the hue says nothing. Status colours were not changed.

Typography

Noto Sans. Body 14px / 1.5. Section titles 20px/700. Modal titles 18–20px/700. Table headers 13px/400 in mid grey. Small uppercase labels 11px/700.

Geometry

  • Radius: 8px buttons, 10–12px inputs, 12px cards, 14px modals.
  • Buttons: primary blue filled; outline on white. 36px in table rows, 44px in forms.
  • Inputs 42–44px, with a blue focus ring.
  • Cards: white, 1px border, 12px radius. Table cells 14px padding.
  • Badges: 3px / 8px, 12px radius, 12px text, never wrapping.
  • Icons: 24×24 grid, 2px stroke, round caps and joins.

The two deliberate changes to video

Everything else leaves a video-only event looking exactly as it did. These two don't, on purpose:

  1. A video-only event now shows an Interview location column on the Interviewing tab, reading “Video” on every row. Consistency was chosen over density.
  2. The video chip moved from blue to the shared chip colour.
