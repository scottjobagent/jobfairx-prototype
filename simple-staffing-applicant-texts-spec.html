01

Summary and scope

Simple Staffing posts its jobs through Workable; they run on Indeed and on the other boards Workable feeds. Each job has one interview day with a time window, almost always 11:00 AM to 3:00 PM, and a fixed number of interview slots. Every applicant gets up to three texts from (762) 465-9036 asking them to request an interview time on JobFairX; the employer then accepts or declines the request. Nobody reads replies; Twilio answers STOP and HELP.

In scope: the three texts and their merge fields; when each is sent and the checks before every send; the Twilio setup; the page the link opens; the resume step; logging and the weekly report.

Out of scope: wording tests (they live in the plan), the Workable email and the website Contact page (section 13, outside the code), JobFairX’s reminder emails after an employer accepts (unchanged), and RCS (later).

Words used here. A request is the applicant picking a time on JobFairX; the employer still has to accept it. The closing moment is defined in section 5. A held text failed a send check at the moment of sending. Applicant-local means the applicant’s own time zone; job time means the employer’s.

The Text 1 test

Two openers run side by side from launch, because the opener in front of 70% of all requests has a year of results behind it and the brand-first line has none.

  • Assignment: the last digit of the applicant’s phone number, even gets A and odd gets B, decided once when Text 1 is prepared and stored with the application. A person who applies twice gets the same opener both times.
  • Opener A, today’s: “Hi {First name}, my name is Scott with Simple Staffing. I received your application for the {Job title} job on {Source site}.” Opener B, brand first: “Simple Staffing: Hi {First name}, we received your application for the {Job title} job on {Source site}.” Everything after that sentence is identical, and Texts 2 and 3 are identical in both arms.
  • Logging: the opener goes on every text row and application row (section 10); the weekly report shows the request rate and the STOP rate by opener.
  • Size and length: run to 590 applicants per opener, about 40 days at the current pace, which shows an 8-point difference. Read it at the end, not along the way.
  • Guardrail: if one opener’s STOP rate after Text 1 runs at twice the other’s over at least 200 texts, stop the test and keep the lower one.
  • Decision rule, set now: a gap under 3 points keeps B; a larger gap keeps the winner. Scott reads the result; Brandon then removes the losing opener.

05

Send rules

Before every send

Every text is checked at the moment it is handed to Twilio, not when it was scheduled. All six must pass:

  1. The applicant has not requested a time.
  2. The number has not opted out (any STOP, for any job).
  3. Interview slots are open.
  4. The job is open: not paused, closed or cancelled.
  5. The closing moment has not passed.
  6. This text number has not already been sent for this application.

A text that fails is held and logged with one of the reasons in section 10.

The closing moment

Requests for a job close when its interview slots are full and reopen if the employer adds slots. They close while the job is paused and reopen when it is unpaused. They close for good when the job is closed or cancelled, and they close for good four hours before the interview window starts: 7:00 AM job time for an 11:00 AM window. After the closing moment no text goes out and the link opens the page that says times are no longer available. When slots fill, the recruiter gets an email and decides whether to add slots, and the Indeed posting pauses on its own and comes back when slots are added; both are already in place.

Held texts and reopening

A text held for SLOTS_FULL or JOB_PAUSED is sent when the job reopens: at once if the applicant’s window is open and the closing moment has not passed, otherwise at 8:00 to 8:30 AM the next morning. Texts 2 and 3 are then scheduled from the day Text 1 actually went out. A text held for any other reason is never sent.

Clocks and zones

  • Applicant time zone: from the state or province, or the ZIP or postal code, on the application; if missing, from the phone’s area code; if the area code spans two zones or is not geographic, use the job’s time zone. Store zones by IANA name, never as a fixed offset, so daylight time, Arizona and Saskatchewan work. Keep the server’s time-zone data at IANA 2026c or later: British Columbia stays at UTC-7 all year since 8 March 2026.
  • Oregon: when the application’s state is Oregon or the area code is 503, 971, 541 or 458, the window ends at 7:45 PM and no number gets more than three automated texts in any 24 hours (ORS 646A.372).
  • Canada: Canadian applicants get the same texts, schedule and 8:00 AM to 8:45 PM window in their own zone. Texts fall under CASL, whose regulations exempt a message sent in response to the person’s own request (Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations, s. 3(b)); a text answering their application fits that on a plain reading, and counsel can confirm. If counsel says CASL applies in full, the request page adds Simple Staffing’s mailing address and an email or phone number.
  • Interview day: nothing is sent on the interview day except a Text 1 that still clears the closing moment in job time.
  • Twilio: hand every message over with ValidityPeriod 900 seconds, so a queued text can never arrive after 9:00 PM (8:00 PM Oregon).

Failures, duplicates and opt-outs

  • Failures: a text Twilio rejects, or later marks failed or undelivered, is logged with its error code and not retried. The next scheduled text runs its own checks.
  • Duplicates: an application gets each text number at most once. Each application runs its own sequence; a person with two applications gets two sequences.
  • Opt-outs: Twilio’s opt-out handling answers STOP and HELP. The app records every STOP and never texts that number again for any job, as it does today; the app keeps its own record because Twilio applies a STOP per sending number or service.

06

Characters and length

One character outside the GSM-7 set turns a 3-segment text into 6. Job titles and company names from Workable often carry curly quotes and en dashes, so every merge value is normalised before the template is filled.

  • Curly single quotes to a straight apostrophe; curly double quotes to a straight quote; en dash, em dash and minus sign to a hyphen; the ellipsis character to three periods; non-breaking and other Unicode spaces to a normal space.
  • Emoji and any other character outside GSM-7 are removed. Accented letters inside GSM-7 (for example é) stay.
  • Log any message that would still send as UCS-2, and keep the templates themselves to straight apostrophes and hyphens.
  • Measured with the sample values (first name Jordan, source Indeed) and a 30-character link: Text 1 is 415 characters (video and phone) or 443 (in person) with opener A and 394 or 422 with opener B; Text 2 is 400 or 428; Text 3 is 447 or 475. Texts 1 and 2 are 3 segments in every version, and so is Text 3 for video and phone; Text 3 for an in-person job is 4 segments. Text 2 for an in-person job stays at 3 segments even with a 39-character title and a 30-character company (443). Dropping the source site words shortens a text by 10 characters with Indeed.
  • Test-send one message with the company “Baylor Scott & White Health” and confirm the ampersand arrives intact; some platforms escape it.

08

The request page and the link

  • The link: on jobfairx.com, the name the texts use; https included, 39 characters or fewer; unique per applicant per text; never a public shortener, and Twilio link shortening and click tracking stay off. After the closing moment it opens the page that says times are no longer available; it never says the date has passed while the interview date is still ahead.
  • Above the fold: Simple Staffing, the employer name and the job title, with a line such as “Simple Staffing schedules its interviews on JobFairX”, so the applicant sees the same facts the text gave them before anything else.
  • Footer: Simple Staffing’s contact email scott@simplestaffing.co, the texting number (762) 465-9036, and JobFairX’s privacy policy link. Carrier rules (CTIA, AT&T) require a linked page to name its owner, with a privacy policy where it collects personal information.

09

Resume step

  • When the Workable application already carries a resume, attach it to the request and skip Upload a resume and the resume builder. The employer’s requirement itself stays. Workable’s candidate webhook carries resume_url; the app fetches the file from there.
  • When the employer marks the resume as not required for a job, hide the resume steps in that job’s request flow.

10

Logging and the weekly report

Record these from the first send, so the request rate can be reported every week and split by job, employer, format, board and text version.

Per text sent: application, text number (1, 2 or 3), template version, planned send time, actual send time in UTC and in the applicant’s local time, the number it was sent from, the held reason if any, Twilio message id, Twilio status and error code, segment count, interview format, source site, Text 1 opener (A or B).

Per application: job, employer, interview date, time applied, source site, Text 1 opener, resume source (attached from Workable, uploaded, built, or skipped), when the link was first opened and which text it followed, when a request was made and which text it followed (the last text sent before it), STOP time and the exact reply wording, and every other reply with its time and wording.

Weekly report: applicants texted, requests and the request rate (requests divided by applicants texted), requests by the text they followed, texts held by reason, link opens divided by delivered, delivered, undelivered and failed counts, texts with no delivery receipt by sending number, STOP replies per text, other replies received, the share of texts at 4 segments, and the request rate by job, by employer, by format, by days before the interview, by board, US against Canada, and Text 1 opener A against B, with the STOP rate by opener.

11

Acceptance checks

Run before the switch, however Brandon tests. Each is pass or fail.

  1. Apply to an Indeed job at 3:00 PM applicant time. Text 1 arrives 15 minutes later from (762) 465-9036 through the Event Notifications service, 3 segments, GSM-7, the Video wording, “on Indeed”.
  2. Apply at 9:30 PM. Text 1 arrives between 8:00 and 8:30 AM the next morning, applicant time.
  3. Apply from an Oregon number at 7:50 PM. Text 1 waits for the next morning, and no number gets more than three texts in 24 hours.
  4. Apply through Monster: the text says “on Monster”. Apply through Workable’s own board: the text says “for the {Job title} job.” with no site named.
  5. In-person job: Text 1 names the city and Text 3 is 4 segments. Phone job: the phone sentence and the request line that names JobFairX.
  6. A job title with an en dash and curly quotes: the text sends as GSM-7, 3 segments, and the log shows no UCS-2 send.
  7. Company “Baylor Scott & White Health”: the ampersand arrives intact.
  8. Request a time after Text 1: no Text 2 or Text 3; the log shows REQUESTED for the skipped sends.
  9. Reply STOP after Text 1: no further text to that number for any job; the STOP is logged with its wording.
  10. Fill the job’s slots: the next text is held with SLOTS_FULL. Add a slot: the held Text 1 goes out within the window and Texts 2 and 3 follow from that day.
  11. Pause the job: texts hold with JOB_PAUSED and resume when it is unpaused. Close the job: nothing more is sent, ever.

Decisions made

Each decision is written as the spec applies it, with the date it was made.

  • 1. Applying two days before the interview: Text 2 lands the day before and is sent anyway, because the request is the goal. (7 Oct)
  • 2. Text 3 for an in-person job is 4 segments. The cost is accepted rather than cutting the greeting or the title. (7 Oct)
  • 3. Time zone is written as two letters (CT), not the daylight form (CDT). (7 Oct)
  • 4. The closing moment: requests close when slots are full and reopen if slots are added, close while a job is paused, close for good when a job is closed or cancelled, and close for good four hours before the interview window starts. No text goes out after it. (18 Oct)
  • 5. The city field holds the city only. (7 Oct)
  • 6. Jobs run in the US and Canada; Canadian applicants get the same texts, schedule and window in their own time zone. (17 Oct)
  • 7. Text 1 opens two ways from launch, tested side by side: A as today, “Hi {First name}, my name is Scott with Simple Staffing. I received…”, and B brand first, “Simple Staffing: Hi {First name}, we received…”. Texts 2 and 3 open “Simple Staffing: Hi {First name},” and name no person. A gap under 3 points keeps B. (18 Oct)
  • 8. When slots fill, the recruiter gets an email and decides whether to add slots; the Indeed posting pauses on its own and comes back when slots are added (both in place); the app holds every text to that job while slots are full and resumes when they reopen. (17 and 18 Oct)
  • 9. Text 1 says “{Company} is interviewing for this job on {Interview date}” and Text 2 “is still interviewing”, not “would like to interview you”. (18 Oct)
  • 10. Every text names the board the applicant used; Workable’s own board is never named and then the site words are dropped. (18 Oct)
  • 11. The interview window comes from the job as two merge fields, not fixed text. (18 Oct)
  • 12. A text Twilio rejects or fails is logged and not retried; the next scheduled text runs its own checks. (18 Oct)
  • 13. A STOP blocks every future text to that number for any job (in place today). (18 Oct)
  • 14. Each application runs its own full sequence; a person with two applications gets two sequences. (7 Oct)
  • 15. Three texts, never four. Text 3 is the final reminder, sent three days before the interview whatever the lead time, and it names the application date. A day-3 text and a fourth text were set aside until the weekly report shows how applicants with long lead times respond. (18 Oct)
