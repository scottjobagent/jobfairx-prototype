Leads into the admin

Leads for the sales dashboard come from Scott's Gmail. A script on his Google account posts every new address he emails to the admin; the admin stores them, shows them on a Leads page Scott cleans up daily, and counts them on the dashboard the way HubSpot did. What you build: POST /leads, the Leads table and page, the dashboard counts, and the website form hook.

01

Goal

HubSpot is being cancelled. Its lead count was the number of new contacts per day, and nearly all of those contacts were created by its Gmail extension the moment Scott emailed someone. The admin takes that over. A lead is a new email address Scott writes to (To or Cc), or a website employer form submission. ActiveCampaign is not used. The admin is the CRM: a Leads table, a Leads page where Scott deletes the non-leads each day, and the New Leads tiles on the sales dashboard, which already match HubSpot's layout.

02

Where leads come from: the Gmail script

A Google Apps Script runs under scott@jobfairx.com every 10 minutes (project "JobFairX Leads", written 21 Oct 2026, already running in log-only mode). What it does:

  • Reads the mail Scott sent since the last run, with a 30-minute overlap so nothing is missed.
  • Every To or Cc address becomes a lead the first time it appears. The script keeps its own list of addresses it has already sent, so each address is posted once; the admin's one-per-email rule is the second guard.
  • Skips our own domains (jobfairx.com, jobfairxjobs.com, simplestaffing.co), our team's other addresses, candidate addresses (indeedemail.com, indeed.com) and anything under Scott's Applicant Replies label, and system mailboxes (noreply, notifications, support, billing and the like) at known vendors (Eventbrite, Workable, Google, LinkedIn, Twilio, SendGrid and others).
  • Name: from Gmail's To line when it has one, otherwise a guess from a first.last address. Company: a guess from the email domain, or from the person's signature when the address is Gmail, Yahoo and the like. Both are guesses, so both are editable on the Leads page.
  • Posting is off until the endpoint and token are in; until then it logs to a Google Sheet in Scott's Drive, which stays as a backup log afterwards. A post that fails is retried on the next run.
  • Volume: a handful per 10 minutes at most, typically 10 to 30 leads a day.
  • The script never deletes anything and never reads the admin. Scott's daily Delete on the Leads page is the only way a non-lead comes out.

05

Leads page in the admin

  • A Leads tab of its own in the admin. Desktop only and no export, like the other admin pages.
  • Newest first: Created (Pacific date and time), Name, Email, Company, Source (Gmail or Website form), Subject linking to the thread in a new window.
  • Delete on every row: one click, no confirmation, soft delete. The row greys out with an Undo link until the page reloads, and a "Show deleted" filter brings deleted rows back with an Undelete.
  • Name and Company are editable in place; an edit sets company_source to "manual".
  • Search by name, email or company. Filters: date range with a Today shortcut (Scott's daily check), source, show deleted.
  • A count line above the list: "N leads today", live rows only.

07

Website employer form

Create the lead straight from the submission: source "website_form", company from the form's company field with company_source "manual", counted when it arrives even if Scott never replies. Keep emailing Scott the notification as today. Same table and the same one-per-email rule, so a later reply from Scott finds the address and makes no second lead.

08

Token, environments and switch-on

  • Generate a long random token (32 characters or more), keep it in the admin config, and send it to Scott directly, never through chat with Claude. A staging URL and a test token first, so the script can dry-run; then the live URL and token.
  • Scott's side, two clicks in the script project: Project settings, Script properties, add ENDPOINT and TOKEN; then run enablePosting. He can stop posting any time with disablePosting.
  • To rotate the token, send Scott the new one and he updates the property. Nothing else changes.

09

Acceptance criteria

  • A POST with a new email returns 201, and the lead shows on the Leads page within a minute with name, company, source, subject and a working thread link.
  • The same POST again returns 200 and makes no second row and no change to created_at.
  • A POST with a bad token returns 401; a POST without an email returns 400.
  • Deleting a lead removes it from every count at once; Undo brings it back.
  • A repeat POST of a deleted lead stays deleted.
  • New Leads Today, Daily, Weekly and Monthly Leads and the two Cycle tiles match a hand count of the Leads table for the same days in Pacific time.
  • A website form submission appears as a lead with source Website form before Scott replies.
  • The Leads page has no export and works at desktop width.
