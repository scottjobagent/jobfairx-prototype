JobFairX — Job Seeker Experience

Dashboard UX audit

A design, workflow and accessibility review of all ten dashboard states, benchmarked against how Ashby, Greenhouse, Lever, Indeed and LinkedIn handle the same problems.

August 16, 2026 · Audit only, nothing implemented · Measured from the running implementation, not from screenshots

1 Executive summary

The dashboard is a table of records. The job seeker's actual need is "what do I do next?" That single mismatch explains almost every problem below. The interface is organised around what the system knows — event, job, status, actions — rather than around what the person must do. Every enterprise product in the benchmark set made this shift years ago: Ashby and Greenhouse lead with the next required action and demote the record to supporting detail.

The recent interview-type work is genuinely good and should be protected. The method line is honest, the phone treatment correctly offers no button, and in-person leads with directions. That layer is not the problem.

The three findings that matter most:

  • Unregister appears in all nine states — including In interview. You can leave the entire hiring event while you are sitting in the interview. It is also styled identically to Cancel interview, despite one dropping a single interview and the other removing you from the event and every match at it.
  • Two states offer exactly one action, and it is Unregister. Waiting to be matched and Request declined are the two most emotionally loaded moments in the product, and the only thing the interface offers is to leave.
  • Keyboard accessibility is effectively broken. Seven outline:none rules, zero :focus-visible, and no visible focus on any action. Combined with 20 non-semantic clickables and no <main>, no <h1>, no scope on any table header and no aria-live, this would not pass an enterprise procurement review.
The one change with the most leverage

Invert the Actions column. Today it lists everything available, flat, in source order. It should surface one next action, with everything else behind progressive disclosure. That single change fixes the 7× density swing, the destructive-scope collision, and the "what do I do next" problem at the same time.

Design system integrity

18 font sizes including 13.5, 12.5 and 11.5 sitting alongside 13, 12 and 11 — differences no reader can perceive, but which guarantee misalignment. 107 bold declarations (weights 600 and 700) against 21 regular: when everything is bold, nothing is emphasised. 66 colours including at least eight near-identical greys.

4 Actions redesign — the core proposal

The principle

One next action, always in the same place, plus disclosure. This is what every product in the benchmark set does. The seeker should never scan a list to work out what to do — the interface should have already decided.

Three tiers

TierTreatmentRule
PrimaryOne filled button, fixed positionExactly one per row, or none. Interview-type aware: Join interview / Open Microsoft Teams / Get directions / none for phone.
SupportingMax one inline linkOnly if it materially helps right now. Test camera & mic for JobFairX video; Message employer once confirmed.
OverflowIcon button opening a menuEverything else. Destructive items separated by a divider, at the bottom, scope-labelled.
Why an overflow menu, and why not the alternatives

Reuse over invention. The employer lobby already solved this exact problem with a primary action plus a kebab menu in one row. Adopting the same component is the single biggest consistency win available between the two products, and it arrives pre-tested.

Split buttons fail here. The primary differs per interview type and for phone there is none at all. A split button with an empty main half is broken by construction.

Action cards are too heavy for a row already carrying event, job, status, time and method. They would suit a detail view, not this.

What each state becomes

Net effect on the worst case: seven flat items become one button, one link and one menu. The single-action states stay clean because the menu only renders when it has contents.

Interaction model

  • Overflow opens on click, closes on outside click and Esc, returns focus to its trigger.
  • Full keyboard support: arrow keys within the menu, Enter/Space to activate.
  • Destructive items sit below a divider and name their scope — "Unregister from this event", never bare "Unregister".
  • Every control becomes a real <button> with a visible :focus-visible ring.

5 Card and layout recommendations

Reconsider the table itself

A four-column table is built for scanning many similar rows. A job seeker typically has one to three interviews. Below 768px it already collapses to cards — and the card layout is measurably clearer than the table. Recommendation: make the card the primary presentation at all widths, with the table reserved for a future "all interviews" view if the volume ever justifies it. This is the LinkedIn and Indeed pattern for "my applications."

Information order within a card

OrderElementReasoning
1Status pill + timeAnswers "what's happening" and "when" first. Currently third.
2Job title + employerThe thing the seeker cares about. Currently second — keep.
3Interview type + its detailAddress, number or link. Already good; keep adjacent to the time.
4Next-step lineNew. One sentence: what happens next, or what to do.
5Action zonePrimary + supporting + overflow, right-aligned, fixed position across all states.
6Event nameDemote. It is context, not the subject. Currently occupies the leading column.
The event-name demotion is the biggest hierarchy change

Today "Dallas Healthcare Hiring Event" is the first and most prominent thing in the row. But the seeker is not managing events — they are managing interviews. The job and the employer are the subject; the event is filing metadata. Every benchmark product leads with the role and company.

Design system cleanup

  • Type scale: 18 sizes down to 7 — 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 20, 26. Drop every half-pixel value.
  • Weights: three only — 400, 600, 700 — and use 700 sparingly enough that it means something.
  • Greys: 66 colours down to a 5-step neutral ramp plus the semantic set. Retire #9a9794 and #b0b0b0 outright, since both fail contrast.
  • Radii: 13 values down to four — 6 (controls), 10 (cards), 12 (modals), 9999 (pills).

Decisions I need before implementing

  • Card-first layout (item 7) — a significant visual change to shipped work. In or out?
  • Removing Unregister from the row entirely versus keeping it in the overflow. I recommend the overflow, scope-labelled.
  • Expected response time — is there a real number for how quickly employers respond? If not I will use a placeholder rather than invent one.
  • Preparation layer — worth building, or out of scope for now?
